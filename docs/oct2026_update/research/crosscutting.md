## 2. Candidate new dimensions

### ADD: Immigration-enforcement climate (international and immigrant attendees)
- **Key:** `ilrc_stateImmigrationEnforcement_2026`
- **URL:** https://www.ilrc.org/state-map-immigration-enforcement (updated Sept 6 2026). Data comes from the embedded Datawrapper chart https://datawrapper.dwcdn.net/6SaKX/14/dataset.csv
- **Rows:** 51 (50 states + DC).
- **Method:** ILRC (Immigrant Legal Resource Center) scored 20 parameters of state law from 1 (most harmful) to 5 (most protective). Absent laws count as 3. Litigation that blocked a law raises its score from 1 to 2. The parameters are averaged ("Rounded Score (2026)").
- **Scoring:** `Processed = (avg - 1)/4 * 10`, so higher means more protective. The observed range is TX 1.6 → 1.5 and IL/OR 4.3 → 8.25. The raw average and a litigation flag are kept as extra columns.
- **Proposed P = 3:** an advocacy organization, but its method is transparent and law-based.
- **Proposed category:** new `immigration` (or `international_access`).
- **Fit and caveats:**
  - This is directly relevant now: societies (AOM, ISRA, CO3, ICM petition) are citing it, and the DPP has many non-US attendees.
  - It covers state *legislation* only. Federal ICE/CBP activity applies everywhere, so it captures the marginal state effect and not visa or border risk.
  - It also misses city-level sanctuary policies (ILRC's separate county map covers those).
  - Spearman correlation with HRC SEI 2024 is 0.82, so it partly tracks overall state policy climate.
- Raw files: `data/raw_data_2026/ilrc_stateImmigrationEnforcement_2026/`

### ADD (or MAYBE): Gun-law strength (personal safety)
- **Key:** `giffords_gunLawScorecard_2025`
- **URL:** https://giffords.org/scorecard/ (press release Dec 8 2025: https://giffords.org/press-release/2025/12/giffords-releases-annual-gun-law-scorecard-data-shows-stronger-gun-laws-save-lives/)
- **Rows:** 50.
- **Scoring:** `Rank` is the Giffords gun-law-strength rank, where 1 = strongest laws = best (CA 1, WY 50; ties exist, e.g., 30, 36×3). No inversion was needed. Extra columns are `Grade` and `GunDeathsPer100k` (Giffords' CDC-based figure; the data year is not stated on the page). The data was parsed from the page's `data-*` attributes and matches the press release (24 states graded F, CA top, WY bottom).
- **Proposed P = 3:** advocacy organization, long-running and transparent.
- **Proposed category:** new `safety`.
- **Caveats:**
  - It measures laws, not visitor risk. Gun deaths are dominated by suicide, so the rank is used instead of the death rate.
  - Spearman correlation with HRC is 0.82 (partly redundant with the political-climate signal).
  - AGU explicitly lists gun safety as a monitored issue.

### MAYBE: Health-care access for travelers
Commonwealth Fund 2025 State Health System Performance Scorecard (June 18 2025; 50 states + DC; MA, HI, NH, RI, DC top): https://www.commonwealthfund.org/publications/scorecard/2025/jun/2025-scorecard-state-health-system-performance
- **No dataset produced.** The site blocks automated fetches (403/Cloudflare), and the scorecard is resident-oriented (insurance, prevention), so it fits visitors weakly.
- The traveler-relevant part, emergency obstetric care under abortion bans, is covered by the gender agent. Note the gender agent already produced `commonwealthFund_womensHealthReproCare_2024`.

### NO: Hate-crime rates (FBI UCR/NIBRS)
- Participation is voluntary, and most participating agencies report zero incidents. State totals reflect reporting practice: CA reports the most, MS almost none (third-party tally, snippet).
- The BJS victimization survey estimates far more incidents than the FBI counts.
- Using these numbers would reward non-reporting states. If cited at all, use only the HRC MEI "reports hate crimes to FBI" item, as the American Philosophical Assoc. does.
- Ref: https://www.propublica.org/article/more-100-federal-agencies-fail-report-hate-crimes-fbi-national-database

### NO (describe only): Religious-minority safety
- ADL Audit 2025 (https://www.adl.org/resources/report/2025-audit-antisemitic-incidents) reports 6,274 incidents, with state counts such as NY 1,160, CA 817, NJ 687. These are raw counts driven by population and reporting.
- No equivalent 50-state source exists for anti-Muslim or other faiths (CAIR data is complaint-based).
- The religion dimension is already inside the OBI Inclusiveness Index (`religion_subindex`).

### NO: Immigration via ICE 287(g) agreement counts
- ICE publishes these officially, but counts change weekly (2,510 agreements by Sep 2026 per secondary reports), are county-driven and depend on population. The ILRC state-law score is the better state-level measure.

### Also considered, not recommended as inputs
- Grumbach State Democracy Index 2.0 (2000-2023; https://democracypolicylab.berkeley.edu/state-democracy-index/): measures democratic performance, not attendee experience.
- SmileHub "Best States for Immigrants" 2026: seen only in secondary coverage; low-transparency publisher.

## 3. Meta-indices (cross-checks)
- **OBI (Othering & Belonging Institute, UC Berkeley) Inclusiveness Index 2025:** covers race, gender, LGBTQ, religion, disability and general population (outgroup violence, political representation, income inequality, anti-discrimination law, incarceration). 2025 top three: HI, NH, DE. URL: https://belonging.berkeley.edu/inclusiveness-index ; https://www.otheringandbelonging.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Oct.-22-2025-enews.html
  - **This is the closest existing analogue to GARDN-M.** Another agent has already produced `berkeleyOBI_inclusivenessIndex_2025.csv`.
  - **Recommendation:** use it EITHER as an input OR as an independent cross-check, not both. Correlating GARDN-M's output with an index it was built from is circular.
- **Georgetown U.S. Women, Peace & Security Index:** inclusion, justice and security dimensions; 50 states + DC. https://giwps.georgetown.edu/u-s-wps-index-methodology/ (gender-focused; the gender agent owns it).
- **Commonwealth Fund State Scorecard 2025:** health-system composite (URL above).
- **Cato "Freedom in the 50 States" (2026 edition):** https://www.cato.org/freedom-50-states
  - It is a libertarian construct: its personal-freedom component counts looser gun laws as *more* free, so it runs opposite to the Giffords score.
  - **Useful only as a contrast and not as an equity cross-check.**
- **Grumbach State Democracy Index 2.0:** https://democracypolicylab.berkeley.edu/state-democracy-index/
- **Social Progress Index for US states:** last full state edition found was 2018/2020 (https://www.socialprogress.org). Outdated.

## 4. Suggested text for "Considering Laws or Recent Developments"
- **Travel-ban lists:** add that California's AB 1887 state-funded travel-ban list, a common de facto exclusion list, was repealed by SB 447 on 13 Sep 2023. No official multi-state "do not travel" list exists any more, so committees must do their own legal review.
- **Practice at peer societies:** cite the shift from ad hoc relocation to written policies, all of which require contract flexibility:
  - AHA: cancellation-without-penalty clauses.
  - SPSP 2026: force majeure for "the passing of laws".
  - ACNP 2023: no contracting where discriminatory laws exist "at the time of contracting".
  - ACHA 2026: laws "may inform, but shall not solely govern".
- **Recommended contract terms:** add that organizers should negotiate a force-majeure or "change-in-law" clause, since GARDN-M inputs can go stale between contract signing and the meeting.
- **New dimension, international attendees:** note that since 2025 the largest new driver of relocations is entry, visa and immigration-enforcement risk for international attendees (Physics Today 2025: attendance drops of about 20-30% at some physics meetings; AOM moving 2027-2030 abroad; ICM 2026 petition). This is mostly *federal* and cannot be fixed by choosing a state. GARDN-M should therefore pair any state-level immigration score (ILRC) with hybrid/virtual or non-US satellite options, as APS already offers (virtual component, Hong Kong satellite).
- **Physical safety:** note that it is now an explicit criterion in several policies (SOBP: low crime; SPSP: GeoSure; AGU: gun safety; APS 2020: policing). Hate-crime statistics should not be used as a safety ranking because reporting is voluntary and incomplete.
- **Validation:** recommend checking GARDN-M output against an independent composite, the OBI Inclusiveness Index, and reporting disagreements. Only do this if OBI is not also an input.
- **Advisory list:** list the advisories (NAACP and LULAC Florida 2023, CAUT US-travel advisory 2025) as examples of fast-moving signals that committees should check at decision time and again before the event.

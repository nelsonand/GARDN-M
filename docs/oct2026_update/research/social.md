## Existing sources
- **infogram_socialEquityCitiesRanking**: this is Motley Fool Money's "Most Socially Equitable Cities in the U.S." (https://www.fool.com/money/research/most-socially-conscious-cities/). The page says "Updated Dec. 16, 2024", but the embedded Infogram table (same URL, infogram updatedAt 2024-11-05) has identical numbers to our existing CSV (SF 74.74, Seattle 72.78, ...). The data underneath is still 2019-2020 (e.g. FBI 2019 crime, ACS 2019). **There is no new edition, so keep the old file.** It is effectively stale and could reasonably be down-weighted.
- **nationalEquityAtlas** (PolicyLink/USC ERI Racial Equity Index): **could not refresh.** nationalequityatlas.org returns HTTP 403 to automated requests (curl and WebFetch), and the Internet Archive was offline/rate-limited. Search found no evidence of a new edition of the index after its July 2020 launch, but I could not check this on the site. A user with a browser could check https://nationalequityatlas.org/research/racial_equity_index/index for a state/city download.

## Candidates found but not extracted
- Social Progress Imperative, US Social Progress Map (50 states, 500 cities). https://www.socialprogress.org/us ; Oct 2025 post naming the top 5 as MD, CT, MN, NH, MA: https://www.socialprogress.org/post/discover-the-best-places-to-live-in-america-top-five-u-s-states . The data sits behind an interactive/premium tool and the methodology PDF is image-only. This would be a strong P=5 source if the data can be obtained (it also has city-level data, which could replace the stale infogram city source).
- Brookings Metro Monitor 2026 (inclusion and racial inclusion ranks for metros, 2014-2024). https://www.brookings.edu/articles/metro-monitor-2026/ . The raw JSON is public (…rackcdn.com/interactives/2026/metro-monitor/assets/data/ranks.json, values.json). Not used because its ranks measure 10-year *change*, not current levels, and are ranked within size classes (e.g. 55 "very large" metros), so they do not fit GARDN's Rank/Score format.
- OBI city-level Black-white disparities data: https://belonging.berkeley.edu/obi-publishes-city-level-data-black-white-disparities . This is race-specific, so better suited to the race category.
- SDSN US States SDG Index: https://us-states.sdgindex.org/rankings . Appears to be an old edition and was not pursued.
- Measure of America American HD Index: no state edition after about 2013-14 was found.

## Summary / landscape reports
- OBI, Inclusiveness Index 2025 (Oct 2025), https://belonging.berkeley.edu/inclusiveness-index . Hawaii is again the most inclusive state. Every state is scored on race, gender, LGBTQ, religion, disability and general inclusion. Note: its sub-indices overlap the other GARDN categories, so watch for double counting if other agents also use it.
- Oxfam, Best States to Work in the U.S. 2025 (Aug/Sept 2025), https://www.oxfamamerica.org/statereport2025 . DC, CA, OR and NY lead; NC, MS, AL, GA and TN trail. The South dominates the bottom, and the report finds BSWI scores correlate with lower poverty and food insecurity. It also includes a Best States for Working Women sub-index (Table 2), which may matter for the gender category.
- Archbridge Institute, Social Mobility in the 50 States 2025 (Nov 2025), https://www.archbridgeinstitute.org/social-mobility-in-the-50-states/ . Utah, Vermont, Montana, Wyoming and Idaho rank top; Louisiana, Mississippi, Alabama, New York and Arkansas rank bottom. It cautions against comparing with the 2023 edition because the methodology changed.
- Social Progress Imperative, "Discover the Best Places to Live in America" (Oct 2025), link above. On quality of life, the top states are MD, CT, MN, NH and MA.
- Brookings, Metro Monitor 2026 (2014-2024 trends incl. racial/geographic inclusion), link above.

## Caveats
- Several sources disagree sharply. Archbridge and Oxfam are roughly opposite in ideology (e.g. Utah is 1st in Archbridge and 46th in Oxfam). OBI's overall ranking has some surprises (Massachusetts 48th, New Jersey 50th), driven by indicators such as hate-crime reporting rates.
- The OBI, Oxfam and Census files include DC (Oxfam, Census) or not (OBI, Archbridge). All use Score/Processed, so main.py's 50-row Rank check does not apply.
